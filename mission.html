Our Mission

Science, conservation, community, and education: the four pillars of the Mara Hyena Project.

Mission Statement

The Mara Hyena Project advances scientific understanding of spotted hyena behavior, ecology, and cognition through long-term field research. We work to conserve hyenas and the ecosystem they depend on, to support the communities that share the Maasai Mara with them, and to train the next generation of scientists in Kenya and abroad.

Since 1988, the MHP has carried out daily field research in the Maasai Mara, building one of the most detailed long-term datasets on any wild animal population. Good science and real community partnership are not competing goals. Each depends on the other.

We think long-term studies are irreplaceable. Questions about how early life shapes adult outcomes, how social structures change, or how populations respond to a changing environment cannot be answered within a single grant cycle. More than 35 years of continuous monitoring is what makes this kind of research possible.

We also work to make that science useful beyond the academy: for the communities that share the Mara with hyenas, for the people who shape wildlife management, and for a public that rarely gets accurate information about one of Africa’s most misunderstood animals.

Our Pillars

What Guides Our Work

Science

Rigorous, peer-reviewed research built on one of the longest continuous datasets on any wild mammal. We work in the open, sharing data and findings so others can build on them.

Our research →

Conservation

Our long-term data informs hyena conservation across the wider Mara–Serengeti ecosystem. We collaborate with regional scientists and Kenyan institutions, including the Wildlife Research and Training Institute (WRTI), on shared conservation goals.

Conservation & threats →

Community

We are part of the Maasai Mara. We hire and train Maasai staff and researchers, give talks at lodges, schools, and to local partners, and work directly to reduce human-hyena conflict.

Community outreach →

Education

We train and mentor the next generation of scientists, from American and Kenyan undergraduates to graduate students, and help build STEM capacity in Kenya by supporting young Maasai researchers.

More Than 35 Years in the Mara

Across more than three decades of daily monitoring, the MHP currently follows seven hyena clans in the Maasai Mara. That work depends on a dedicated field and research team, the support of funders and donors, and the partnership of Kenyan communities and institutions. We intend to keep it going.
