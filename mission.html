Our Mission

Science, conservation, and community — the three pillars of the Mara Hyena Project.

Mission Statement

The Mara Hyena Project is dedicated to advancing scientific understanding of spotted hyena behavior, ecology, and cognition through rigorous long-term field research, while contributing meaningfully to the conservation of hyenas and the communities that live alongside them in Kenya’s Maasai Mara.

Since 1988, the MHP has pursued this mission through daily field research in the Maasai Mara, building one of the most detailed longitudinal datasets on any wild animal population. Our work is grounded in a conviction that excellent science and genuine community engagement are not competing priorities — they reinforce each other.

We believe that long-term studies are irreplaceable. Questions about how early life shapes adult outcomes, how social structures evolve, or how populations respond to environmental change cannot be answered in the span of a single grant cycle. The MHP’s 35-year commitment to continuous monitoring is what makes our science uniquely powerful.

We are equally committed to making that science relevant beyond academia — to the communities that share the Mara with hyenas, to policymakers who shape wildlife management decisions, and to a public audience that deserves accurate information about one of Africa’s most misunderstood animals.

Our Values

What Guides Our Work

Scientific Rigor

We hold our research to the highest standards of behavioral ecology and evolutionary biology. Our methods are transparent, our data carefully curated, and our findings subject to peer review.

Long-Term Commitment

Some of the most important questions in ecology can only be answered across years and decades. We are committed to continuous monitoring not just because it produces better science, but because the animals and communities we study deserve consistency.

Community Partnership

The MHP operates in the Maasai Mara because Kenyan communities and institutions permit and support our presence. Providing conflict consulting, running educational programs, and training Kenyan researchers are core activities, not afterthoughts.

Conservation Impact

Our research generates knowledge that directly informs hyena conservation. Understanding population dynamics, threat factors, and human-hyena interactions is the foundation of effective conservation action.

Training the Next Generation

The MHP has trained dozens of graduate students and hundreds of undergraduate researchers. We take mentorship seriously at every level.

Open Science

We publish in peer-reviewed journals, share data where possible, and make our quarterly reports publicly available. Science is most powerful when it is accessible.

35 Years. Two Clans. One Commitment.

The MHP’s longevity reflects the dedication of a remarkable team, the support of funders and donors, and the goodwill of Kenyan communities and institutions. We are grateful — and determined to continue.
