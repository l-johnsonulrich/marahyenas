About the Program

Behavioral Ecology of African Mammals

BEAM is an intensive three-week field course focused on the behavioral ecology of African mammals in their natural habitat. The course curriculum is designed to exploit the extraordinary richness, diversity, and observability of the mammalian fauna inhabiting Kenya's protected areas. The majority of the course is conducted in the Maasai Mara National Reserve.

Situated in the northern portion of the Serengeti ecosystem, the Mara contains savannas, acacia woodlands, and riverine forests, and these habitats are occupied by different groups of mammals. Throughout the course we use our bush vehicles as classrooms and mobile "blinds" from which to observe the behavior of resident herbivores and carnivores. The course also offers students a unique opportunity to learn about the cultures of Kenya, particularly the indigenous Maasai who inhabit the areas surrounding the Mara.

BEAM is offered through Michigan State University's College of Natural Science and the Department of Integrative Biology, and is arranged through the Office for Education Abroad. Once accepted, students enroll for six (6) credits in IBIO 490, Overseas Study in Zoology.

Open to non-MSU students. Students from any institution are encouraged to apply. Non-MSU applicants enroll through MSU's Office for Education Abroad and pay the Lifelong Education rate for tuition (regardless of state of residence). See Eligibility and Costs for details.

Curriculum

Course Objectives

BEAM focuses on the behavior of African mammals and the ecological variables that shape the behavior exhibited by each resident species. During the program, students begin by identifying and becoming familiar with resident mammals, then move on to making detailed observations of individuals and groups and monitoring their interactions. Students learn how the scientific method is applied to the study of behavioral ecology, and use a variety of techniques for systematic data collection to test hypotheses for why each species engages in particular types of behavior.

The best times of day for observing the behavior of African mammals are around dawn and dusk. As a result, students spend two or three hours each morning and evening in vehicles, observing and quantifying the behavior of various mammals and testing hypotheses about why these animals behave as they do. During the warmer hours around midday, and again each evening, course instructors lecture on selected topics germane to understanding the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem and the behavior of its animals.

After developing an understanding of the behavioral biology of African mammals and the methods available for its study, students are expected to develop and conduct independent projects answering specific questions about the behavior of one species, comparing the behavior of two different species, or testing an explanatory hypothesis in relation to behavior.

Topics covered

  • Ecological determinants of mammalian behavior
  • Life history patterns of resident mammals
  • The relationship between morphology and behavior
  • Field methods in the study of mammalian behavior
  • Foraging and feeding behavior
  • Vigilance and anti-predator behavior
  • Reproductive behavior: courtship, sexual behavior, and parental behavior
  • Social behavior: agonistic behavior, affiliative behavior, and communication
  • Physiological correlates of behavior
  • Effects of human populations on behavior and conservation
  • Current research in the field of behavioral ecology

Location

Study Site

The course takes place primarily in the Maasai Mara National Reserve in southern Kenya. The Mara is situated in the northern portion of the Serengeti ecosystem and offers unparalleled opportunities for observation of African mammals.

The vast open, rolling grasslands and riverine forests of the Mara are occupied by an extraordinary diversity of mammalian species, and these animals serve as subjects in the studies conducted by students enrolled in this program. The course also visits other areas within the Great Rift Valley, which afford comparative insights into the behavioral ecology of mammals in different habitats and regions.

Who Can Apply

Eligibility

This program is designed for undergraduate and graduate students majoring in any biological science, environmental science, wildlife ecology, or related fields. Students from other institutions are encouraged to apply for this unique learning experience.

Students must be in good academic standing and have an overall grade point average of at least 3.0 at the time of application. Students must also have completed at least one year of undergraduate courses and an introductory biology course at the time of departure. Priority will be given to students who have successfully completed IBIO 313 (Animal Behavior), IBIO 365 (The Biology of Mammals), IBIO 355 (Ecology) and/or IBIO 415 (Behavioral Ecology), although these courses are not required. Final acceptance into the program is based on an individual interview with the instructors.

Students potentially interested in taking this course should attend an informational meeting held in the fall or early spring semester. Please email one of the instructors if you are interested; they will have more information about the meeting time and place and will be able to add your email address to the BEAM list.

Students who enroll in this course are required to attend at least four additional preparatory meetings to be held during the spring semester. A valid passport, visa, and record of immunizations are required for travel to Kenya.

Please note that not all sites involved in this program are accessible to individuals with certain disabilities. An applicant's participation may also be denied or their participation approval may be revoked if their conduct before departure raises doubts as to their suitability for program participation.

How to Apply

Application

To apply for BEAM, you must complete two separate applications:

1

MSU Office for Education Abroad (OEA) general application

Submit through the OEA website. A $100 non-refundable application fee is required (later applied to the program fee).

2

BEAM Supplemental Application

Download the BEAM Supplemental Application and email your completed form to Jana Woerner.

Deadline: February 15 (per OEA) for both the general and supplemental applications. Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis starting January 1st, and BEAM often fills up long before the OEA deadline due to its small class size (limited to 17 students).

After applications are reviewed, applicants are interviewed by the course instructors and final decisions are made as soon as possible. Applicants are evaluated based on this program's eligibility criteria, overall GPA, GPA in related courses, supplemental application, writing ability, and personal interview.

Detailed information about application procedures, the acceptance process, pre-departure orientation, on-site logistics, and returning home is provided in the Student Handbook (given to students upon submission of their application).

After students accept admission into the program, they will be billed for an additional non-refundable $750 deposit, which is also applied to the program fee. Students who wish to withdraw their application are required to notify the Office for Education Abroad in writing.
