Join the MHP

We welcome volunteer research assistants and prospective graduate students interested in behavioral ecology, cognition, and conservation.

Field Positions

Volunteer Research Assistants

Volunteer RAs join one of our two camps in the Maasai Mara for a minimum of six months and contribute directly to baseline data collection on the Serena North or Talek clans.

What RAs do: Daily observation drives (typically 5–8am and 4–7pm), individual hyena identification by spot pattern, behavioral scoring using standardized protocols, GPS tracking, and camera trap maintenance. RAs also contribute to data entry and quality control.

What RAs gain: Immersive field experience with a wild predator population, training in behavioral observation methods, exposure to long-term ecological research, and the extraordinary experience of living and working in the Maasai Mara.

RA positions are unpaid and self-funded, but the MHP provides accommodation at camp, training, and mentorship. Many former RAs have gone on to graduate programs in behavioral ecology and conservation science.

RA positions are recruited through individual Co-Directors. Review each director’s research focus below and reach out directly to the one whose work aligns with your interests.

Graduate Training

Graduate Students

MHP Co-Directors are based at several universities and accept graduate students through their respective programs. Graduate students typically conduct dissertation research using MHP data and/or field seasons in Kenya, depending on their project.

How to apply

1

Read the research

Familiarize yourself with the MHP’s current projects and publications. Identify which director’s work aligns with your interests. Cold emails without evidence of having read the relevant work are rarely successful.

2

Contact a director directly

Email your director of interest with a brief statement of your background, your research interests, and why you want to work with them specifically. Include your CV. Do not mass-email all directors simultaneously.

3

Apply to their program

Each director admits students through their home institution. Follow that university’s graduate application process. Deadlines typically fall December–January for the following fall.

Life in the Field

What to Expect in the Maasai Mara

Daily Routine

Wake-up is typically before 5am for the morning drive. Observation sessions follow hyena activity, peaking at dawn and dusk. Afternoons involve data entry, vehicle maintenance, and downtime. Flexibility and patience are essential.

Camp Conditions

Both camps are functional but basic. Expect simple accommodations, limited electricity (solar), communal cooking, and no reliable internet. Mobile signal is intermittent. This is a remote research camp, not an eco-lodge.

Health & Safety

Malaria prophylaxis is required. You will work in close proximity to large carnivores — safety protocols are strictly observed. Medical evacuation coverage is mandatory. The MHP provides safety training for all new arrivals.

Community & Culture

Our Kenyan staff are experienced, knowledgeable, and welcoming. Respectful engagement with Maasai culture and community norms is expected. Basic Swahili is helpful but not required.

What You’ll See

The Maasai Mara is one of Africa’s most spectacular ecosystems. Beyond hyenas, you will encounter elephants, lions, cheetahs, wildebeest, and an extraordinary diversity of birds — often within meters of your vehicle.

Physical Demands

Long hours, early mornings, heat, dust, and difficult terrain are part of the job. Good physical fitness, adaptability, and a positive attitude under challenging conditions are more important than any specific prior experience.
