Great expectations? Falling short of status no laughing matter for hyenas

Eli Strauss and Daizaburo Shizuka analyzed MHP data to show that young hyenas who underperformed their inherited social ranks were roughly 1.5 times more likely to die at any given age than those who matched or exceeded expectations.

Ethology — Cover Feature January 2020

Risk-taking in free-living spotted hyenas is associated with anthropogenic disturbance, predicts survivorship, and is consistent across experimental contexts

An MHP paper by Julie Turner, Rachel LaFleur, Allison Richardson, and Kay Holekamp earned the cover of Ethology's January 2020 issue. The study used a life-size model hyena to test responses to a simulated intruder across two populations exposed to different levels of human activity, finding that less time spent near the model predicted better survivorship.

Psychology Today November 26, 2019

Innovation and Fitness Among Wild Female Spotted Hyenas

Marc Bekoff covers a study by Lily Johnson-Ulrich, Sarah Benson-Amram, and Kay Holekamp examining how innovative ability relates to reproductive fitness in MHP's Talek West and Fig Tree clans. The research used a novel puzzle box to test 29 females, finding that more innovative mothers produced more cubs per year despite lower offspring survival.
