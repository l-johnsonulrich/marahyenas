Myths & Misconceptions

Spotted hyenas are among the most misunderstood animals on Earth. Decades of MHP research help set the record straight.

Few animals have suffered more from bad press than the spotted hyena. Portrayed as cowardly scavengers in popular culture — most famously in The Lion King — hyenas are in reality sophisticated predators, devoted parents, and surprisingly complex social creatures.

✘ Myth

Hyenas are scavengers, not hunters

✔ Fact

They are Africa’s most successful predators

Spotted hyenas hunt up to 95% of their own food in some ecosystems. In the Serengeti, research found hyenas were responsible for the kill more than half the time when lions and hyenas shared a carcass — it is often the lions scavenging from hyenas, not the other way around. Their powerful jaws, large hearts, and remarkable endurance make them formidable pursuit predators.

✘ Myth

Hyenas are stupid

✔ Fact

They show remarkable problem-solving and social intelligence

Research by the MHP and other labs has shown that spotted hyenas perform as well as or better than chimpanzees on certain cooperative problem-solving tasks. They live in complex, fission-fusion societies requiring sophisticated social knowledge — each individual must track the identities, ranks, and relationships of dozens of clan members.

✘ Myth

The “laugh” means they find something funny

✔ Fact

It is a complex communication signal

Spotted hyenas have one of the richest vocal repertoires of any carnivore. The giggle is produced during social excitement, fear, or submission — not amusement. Their full repertoire includes whoops (long-distance calls carrying several kilometers), grunts, growls, yells, and soft squeaks between mothers and cubs. MHP research has shown subtle variations carry information about the caller’s identity, age, and rank.

✘ Myth

Hyenas are hermaphrodites

✔ Fact

Females have unique anatomy, but are not hermaphrodites

Female spotted hyenas have an enlarged clitoris called a pseudopenis that closely resembles the male penis, paired with fused labia resembling a scrotum. This unusual anatomy — unique among mammals — makes sex determination by external observation genuinely difficult, and females give birth through the pseudopenis. The evolutionary origins of this trait are still debated; the MHP has contributed research to this question.

✘ Myth

Hyena societies are male-dominated

✔ Fact

Spotted hyena societies are strictly female-dominated

Spotted hyena clans are matriarchies. Every female outranks every immigrant male, and even the lowest-ranking female’s cubs outrank the highest-ranking male from birth. Dominance rank is inherited matrilineally. This unusual social system makes spotted hyenas one of the most striking examples of female dominance in the mammal world, and MHP research has produced foundational work on how and why this system evolved.

✘ Myth

Hyenas are bad parents

✔ Fact

Mothers nurse for up to 18 months and are highly attentive

Spotted hyena mothers invest heavily in their offspring. Cubs nurse for up to 16–18 months, one of the longest lactation periods of any carnivore. Mothers spend more time playing with cubs than most carnivore species. MHP long-term data has been instrumental in documenting how maternal rank and investment affect cub survival and later success.
