About the Project

A Long-Term Study with Lasting Impact

Founded in 1988 by Dr. Kay Holekamp, the Mara Hyena Project has produced some of the most comprehensive data ever collected on a wild mammal population. Our team studies the Serena North and Talek clans in the Maasai Mara Reserve, Kenya.

From social behavior and cognition to the effects of urbanization and climate change, our research addresses fundamental questions about how animals adapt, survive, and thrive in a changing world.

What We Study

Social Behavior

Hyenas live in complex, female-dominated societies. We track social networks, dominance hierarchies, and how early life experiences shape adult outcomes.

Learn more →

Cognition

Spotted hyenas show remarkable problem-solving abilities. We investigate the evolution of intelligence and how environmental pressures shape cognitive traits.

Learn more →

Urbanization

As human settlements expand into wildlife habitats, we study how hyenas respond to and are changed by proximity to people — a natural experiment in rapid evolution.

Community Outreach

Working with Local Communities

The MHP is deeply embedded in the Maasai Mara region. We work alongside Maasai community members, offer free conflict resolution consulting for human-hyena encounters, run educational programs, and provide statistical and ecological training for Kenyan researchers.
