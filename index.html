About the Project

A Long-Term Study with Lasting Impact

Founded in 1988 by Dr. Kay Holekamp, the Mara Hyena Project has produced some of the most comprehensive data ever collected on a wild mammal population. Our team currently studies seven hyena clans in the Maasai Mara National Reserve, Kenya.

From collective behavior, communication, and cognition to development and life history, our research addresses fundamental questions about evolution and adaptation in dynamic environments.

What We Study

Social Systems & Collective Behavior

How do stable societies emerge from individuals with competing interests and diverse experiences? A central focus of the MHP over the past five years has been GPS and acoustic collar studies — fitting entire clans with collars that record location, movement, and sound simultaneously — to understand how hyenas coordinate collective action, communicate across distances, and make group decisions in real time.

Learn more →

Cognition, Communication & Behavior

How do individuals perceive, learn from, and respond to their social and ecological world? We investigate the evolution of cognition and behavioral flexibility, the role of communication in mediating social relationships, and how hyenas navigate the challenges of group living and a rapidly changing environment.

Learn more →

Development, Physiology & Life History

How do early-life social experiences shape biology across a lifetime? We examine how the type and timing of early experiences influence developmental plasticity, stress physiology, and aging, and how the acquisition and allocation of resources drives life history trade-offs and fitness outcomes.

Community Outreach

Working with Local Communities

The MHP is deeply embedded in the Maasai Mara region. We work alongside Maasai community members, hire Maasai staff and researchers, provide outreach and teaching events at local schools, financially support the education of young Maasai scientists, aim to reduce and resolve human-hyena conflict in the region, and provide workshops and talks on a variety of topics from hyena biology to statistical tools for wildlife monitoring.
